Where the hours go

Six places a licensed hour leaks, none of which a job posting fixes.

Every item below is drawn from what Armstrong publishes about itself - the careers listings, the service-area map, the posted office hours, and President Chris Carpenter's own published remarks. The common thread: each one consumes hours from the exact population you cannot hire your way out of.

Why Salesforce, specifically

The question is not which tool dispatches best. It is where the hours come back.

There are good tools built for the service truck, and good tools built for the internal ticket queue. Judged against a labour constraint, both return hours to one department and leave the rest of Armstrong untouched - while the estimator, the dispatcher, the office and the commercial side are all drawing on the same short-handed company.

Category A

Purpose-built for the service truck

Genuinely strong at residential dispatch, call booking and the service invoice. Returns real hours to technicians and dispatchers - and stops there.

  • No model for a bid, submittal, retainage schedule or commercial change order, so estimator hours stay manual
  • Fabrication and VDC workflows live outside the system entirely
  • Marketing is campaign blasts, so office hours still go into building lists by hand
  • Commercial maintenance contracts get forced into a residential job object
  • You extend it by buying another tool - and integrating it costs hours too
Category B

Purpose-built for the ticket queue

Serious workflow and asset management, excellent at modelling process and approvals inside a large enterprise's internal operations.

  • Was not designed for a homeowner deciding on a system replacement on a Saturday
  • No native quote-to-cash or price book, so the technician still cannot quote on site
  • Marketing and demand generation are not in scope at all
  • Implementation weight assumes an internal IT function - which is more headcount you do not have
  • Estimators and CSRs end up back in separate tools, reconciling by hand
The alternative

One platform, hours back across the whole company

Sales, service, field operations, quoting, marketing and analytics on a single customer record - so the hours come back everywhere the shortage bites, not just in the van.

  • Technicians: mobile work orders, guided procedures, auto-drafted summaries
  • Dispatchers: an optimiser that proposes the schedule instead of waiting for it
  • Estimators: guided quoting and versioned commercial bids with margin floors enforced
  • Office and CSRs: an always-on agent covering intake, and a marketing list that builds itself from the record
  • Leadership: utilisation, first-time fix and project margin in one view, no exports
  • Extensible, so fabrication orders and prequal packets become objects rather than more manual work

This comparison is deliberately category-level rather than vendor-specific. We would rather walk a head-to-head against whatever Armstrong is actually evaluating, live, with your team and ours in the room - which is the ask at the bottom of this page.

Agentforce

Four agents, each measured in hours returned to a person you cannot replace.

Not a chatbot on the website. Agents with a defined trigger, a bounded decision and a real action on a real record - each aimed at work currently done by someone whose seat you are trying to fill.

After-Hours Intake Agent
Returns: CSR and on-call hours
TriggerInbound call, chat or form outside posted office hours, in English or Spanish.
DecidesWhether this is a true emergency or a next-business-day call, using the equipment and service history already on the record.
ActsBooks into the live schedule against the on-call rotation and confirms the window to the customer. Escalates to the on-call human with a full transcript when it is out of scope.
Dispatch Copilot
Returns: drive hours and dispatcher rework
TriggerA same-day emergency lands, or a technician runs long and breaks the rest of the day's route.
DecidesWhich jobs to re-sequence and who absorbs the emergency, weighing licence, skill, van stock, territory and the drive-time cost of each option.
ActsProposes the reshuffle for one-click approval, then notifies every affected customer of the new window before they call to ask.
Technician Field Agent
Returns: the evening, and journeyman supervision
TriggerA technician or apprentice opens a work order, or asks a question in plain language from the job site.
DecidesWhat this customer's equipment history, warranty status and prior recommendations imply, and what the right next step and add-on actually are.
ActsDrafts the visit summary, the recommendation and the follow-up quote from the work order - and answers the apprentice question that would otherwise have been a call to a journeyman.
Bid & Prequal Agent
Returns: estimator hours
TriggerA bid invitation or prequalification request arrives from a general contractor or institutional owner.
DecidesWhether the scope fits Armstrong's licences, current crew capacity and historic margin on comparable work, and which prior projects are the strongest evidence.
ActsAssembles the prequal packet from real project data and opens the opportunity with the right stage gates set - work that currently consumes one of two seats you have not filled.
